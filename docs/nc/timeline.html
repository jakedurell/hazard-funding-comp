North Topsail Beach · FEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance

Four properties put forward.
None mitigated.
Nothing filed since 2018.

FEMA mitigation money never reaches a homeowner directly — the town has to apply. North Topsail Beach was told the money carried no local cost, that the state would cover the match, and that it would be paid 5% to administer it. Here is what it did with that, next to what its neighbours did.

Three decades of awards — and where North Topsail Beach stops

Every FEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance award to a North Carolina oceanfront municipality. Each circle is one award, sized by federal dollars obligated and coloured by what it paid for. Hover for detail.

Solid circles are scaled to federal dollars obligated. Open rings are filings that obligated nothing — pending, denied, or closed empty — drawn at a fixed size so an application never disappears just because no money followed it.
Look at 2024. Helene struck western North Carolina, and hazard mitigation money became available statewide. Kure Beach, Southern Shores and Wrightsville Beach each filed — three of the towns on this chart, for a storm that never touched them. So did Jacksonville, and Onslow County, North Topsail Beach's own county. North Topsail Beach's lane is empty.

Everyone else finished what they started

Acquisition, elevation and reconstruction of private property — what a town does when it intends to mitigate somebody's home. Eight of the ten oceanfront towns that put properties forward completed every single one. North Topsail Beach put forward more than any town that delivered none. A dash means the town never proposed a property project, so it has no delivery record either way — different from trying and failing.

Its own county, same table

Every municipality in Onslow County, plus the county itself — the body that applies on behalf of residents when a town does not. Same programmes, same state agency, same deadlines.

Jacksonville's sixteen properties came from a single project, so its 100% reflects one buyout carried through rather than a sustained programme. Onslow County's 43% across ten projects is the steadier number. Both filed in 2024. North Topsail Beach last filed anything in 2018. Surf City appears in both tables — it is oceanfront and it straddles the Onslow–Pender line.

The Reef threw the pass. The town never turned around.

This was not an HOA asking the town to fund its own seawall. The filing position the Reef created in 2024 was one the town could have used for miles of shoreline.

A private HOA paid for the groundwork

Topsail Reef — 239 units, most of them 532 square feet, among the last affordable housing on the island — put more than $2 million of its own money into its sandbag line beginning in 2022, and is still paying interest on the loans. It then approached the state, was told the town had to be the applicant, and spent eighteen months trying to hand the town something it had already built.

The scope was never limited to the Reef

Asked directly whether the project area had limits — whether it could run south to the firehouse and north across other vulnerable homes, taking in the St. Regis buildings — the state's answer was “No.” Coastal engineering favours it: the wider the stretch you protect around a property, the longer the protection holds. As the request itself put it, “HMGP is about more beach than the Reef.”

Flood insurance was not the obstacle

The Reef is NFIP-insured, but the state advised that this was not the operative question for the HMGP route, which carries no property-level flood-insurance requirement. The eligibility test that mattered was the one the town already passed: a current FEMA-approved hazard mitigation plan.

What the town was asked to do

Sponsor the application. Not fund it — the federal share was 75%, the state covered the remaining 25%, and the town would have retained 5% in management costs. A regional council of government had offered to help prepare the paperwork for free. The ask was a motion, an agenda item, a signature.

It never reached a vote. The item was not placed on an agenda. No motion was made. Nothing was filed. The state's second-round window came and went with the project still sitting in NCEM's tracker, waiting on a sponsor that never signed.

The FEMA award data above is public and independently verifiable; the correspondence is quoted from state emails carrying the G.S. 132-1 public-records notice. Weigh the source and check the record.
What this page does not claim. A shore-protection project is not the same as a property buyout, and the two should not be merged. Coastal Barrier Resources System designation covers part of North Topsail Beach and restricts some federal flood insurance, which bears on eligibility for insurance-linked programmes. Neither point explains a town declining to apply for money that carried no local cost.
